The model

A robust cross-classified Bayesian model inspired by Bell et al. (2016) and Andrew Phillips' f1metrics.

Outcome

Each finishing order is transformed to expected Normal order statistics (“rankits”), making results comparable across field sizes. Indianapolis 500 championship rounds are omitted. Mechanical and ambiguous retirements are excluded; accidents and collisions remain driver outcomes.

Structure

rankit ~ StudentT(ν, μ, σ)
μ = driver + constructor + constructor-season
    + shared age curve + recent experience

Driver, constructor and constructor-season effects are partially pooled. Recent experience counts active seasons among the previous four. The constrained age curve peaks between ages 22 and 38. A Student-t likelihood limits the influence of freak results.

What changed

  • Removed starting grid as a predictor: grid is a mediator of driver and car performance, not a pre-race confounder.
  • Replaced the fragile Gaussian likelihood with Student-t residuals.
  • Excluded technical DNFs instead of treating a broken car as driver pace.
  • Constrained age and experience effects to reduce confounding.
  • Publish 94% intervals and conservative ranks, plus convergence diagnostics.

Known limitations

Retirement labels are imperfect. Constructor IDs identify chassis and can conflate customer and works entrants before 1981. Strategy, weather and circuit effects are not yet modeled. Rankings are conditional estimates, not facts.

Sources

Bell, Smith, Sabel & Jones (2016) · Phillips (2019) · F1DB
